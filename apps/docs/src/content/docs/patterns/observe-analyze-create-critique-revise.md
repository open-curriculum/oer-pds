title: "Observe → Analyze → Create → Critique → Revise"
description: A studio-learning pattern that moves learners from example analysis to artifact production, critique, revision, and reflection.
---

**Pattern ID:** `observe-analyze-create-critique-revise`

**Best for:** Studio courses, digital arts, animation, design, media production, writing, portfolio-oriented learning.

## Problem

Learners need to develop both conceptual understanding *and* production judgment — not simply complete a technical task. Without seeing examples first and critiquing work-in-progress, learners often produce artifacts without understanding why their choices work or don't work.

## Sequence

### 1. Observe
Examine curated examples of the skill or concept in context.

- **Learner:** Studies 3–5 provided examples, takes notes on patterns and choices
- **Instructor:** Curates examples, provides observation prompts
- **Evidence produced:** Observation notes

### 2. Analyze
Break down examples using shared vocabulary or criteria.

- **Learner:** Applies vocabulary or analytical criteria to examples, identifies choices and effects
- **Instructor:** Facilitates discussion, provides analytical framework
- **Evidence produced:** Analysis notes, class discussion

### 3. Create
Produce an artifact that applies the analyzed concepts.

- **Learner:** Makes intentional design or production decisions, applies insights from observation
- **Instructor:** Provides brief and constraints, offers feedback during process
- **Evidence produced:** Draft artifact

### 4. Critique
Evaluate own and peers' artifacts using shared criteria.

- **Learner:** Gives and receives structured feedback using critique worksheet or rubric
- **Instructor:** Facilitates critique session, models constructive language
- **Evidence produced:** Critique notes, peer feedback records

### 5. Revise
Improve the artifact based on critique feedback.

- **Learner:** Documents specific revisions, writes revision statement
- **Instructor:** Reviews revision for responsiveness to feedback
- **Evidence produced:** Revised artifact, revision statement

### 6. Reflect
Write a reflection on creative choices, process, and learning.

- **Learner:** Answers reflection prompts, connects learning to future work
- **Instructor:** Grades or responds to reflections
- **Evidence produced:** Reflection

## Assessment Evidence

- Artifact
- Critique notes
- Revision statement
- Reflection

## Example Implementation

**Course:** 3D Animation — Camera Framing

Students observe professional cinematography examples, analyze framing choices using composition vocabulary, produce a rendered shot sequence with intentional camera placement, critique peers' sequences using a critique worksheet, revise their shot, and write a reflection explaining their composition decisions and how they changed after critique.

## AI Guidance

Use this pattern when the course goal includes creative judgment, process documentation, and iterative improvement. Ensure that the critique phase has clear criteria and that revision is assessed for responsiveness to feedback, not just effort.

When generating student-facing materials for this pattern, include:
- Observation prompts that direct attention to specific qualities
- A critique rubric or structured feedback format
- A revision statement template
- Reflection prompts tied to specific creative choices
